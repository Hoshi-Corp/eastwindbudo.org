Kung fu — 功夫, "skill built through effort" — is a name for disciplines that take real time to earn. Ours traces back through the Shaolin Monastery, founded in Henan Province in 495 A.D. by the abbot Ba Tuo, on land granted by the Chinese emperor at the base of Mount Shao Shi. The name is a compound of that mountain and "lin" — forest.

The art's roots run deeper still: Buddhism reached India from Nepal roughly 2,500 years ago, and around 1,500 years ago the monk Bodhidharma carried it into China, founding Chan Buddhism at Shaolin along the way. Shaolin kung fu grew up alongside that practice, built on fluid, animal-derived movement — leopard, dragon, tiger, crane, snake — rather than raw force.
